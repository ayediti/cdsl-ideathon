# Never share an OTP

A **One-Time Password (OTP)** is the bank's way of asking *you* — and only you — to approve something: a payment, a login, a new device, a change to your account.

Whoever has the OTP can approve that action. That is why every OTP message says "do not share".

## The rule

- No genuine bank, broker, depository, regulator, police officer or delivery company will ever ask you for an OTP, PIN or password.
- An OTP never "reverses" a payment or "verifies" your KYC. It authorises money or access going **out**.
- Read the whole OTP message before using it. It usually says what it is for and how much.

## If someone asks

Hang up. Then call your bank yourself, using the number on your card or the bank's official website.
